## Business Overview

Abbott Laboratories is a diversified healthcare company operating across four segments: Diagnostics (30%+ of revenue), Nutrition (20%+), Established Pharmaceuticals (15%+), and Medical Devices (35%+). The company's investment thesis centers on recurring, high-margin revenue from diagnostics, nutrition, and device consumables, supported by structural secular tailwinds: aging populations (chronic disease diagnostic demand), premature infant and pediatric population growth in emerging markets (nutrition demand), and chronic disease prevalence (devices, diagnostics). Abbott's business model generates recurring revenue through: (1) point-of-care testing (blood diagnostics, rapid tests); (2) laboratory automation and instrumentation; (3) pediatric and adult nutritional products; (4) cardiovascular, neuromodulation, and structural heart medical devices.

The company emerged from the 2013 spin-off of AbbVie (pharmaceutical company) as a pure-play medical diagnostics, nutrition, and devices conglomerate. This repositioning has been strategically successful: Abbott has reinvested in higher-growth, recurring-revenue businesses while divesting legacy pharmaceutical assets. The competitive position is strong: Abbott is the market leader in point-of-care diagnostics globally, with installed base switching costs and significant distribution advantages. The nutrition portfolio (Similac, PediaSure, Ensure) is the world's largest by share, with durable pricing power and emerging market growth optionality.

Near-term headwinds include hospital capital equipment spending cycles (devices), reimbursement pressure (diagnostics in developed markets), and supply chain normalization post-pandemic. Medium-term tailwinds include emerging market diagnostic and nutrition adoption, aging-driven cardiovascular and diabetes device demand, and M&A opportunities to acquire bolt-on businesses in high-growth segments. The company is aggressively deploying capital: approximately 70% of free cash flow goes to dividends and buybacks, with remaining cash deployed toward selective inorganic growth.

## Revenue Architecture

Abbott generates revenue across four distinct streams: (1) **Diagnostics** (~$8–9B annually, growing 4–6%): point-of-care testing, laboratory instrumentation and reagents, molecular diagnostics. Margins are 40–45% and relatively stable; this segment has high switching costs (healthcare systems invest in Abbott's instrumentation) and recurring consumables revenue. (2) **Nutrition** (~$5–6B annually, growing 5–8%): pediatric infant formula (Similac), pediatric nutritional supplements, and adult nutrition (Ensure). Margins are 30–35%, supported by consumer brand power and pricing resilience in developed markets; emerging market growth is the long-term tail wind. (3) **Established Pharmaceuticals** (~$4–5B annually, declining 3–5%): includes legacy antibiotics, pain management, and other specialty drugs. This segment is mature and declining due to patent erosion; it is not a growth driver. (4) **Medical Devices** (~$10–12B annually, growing 5–8%): includes cardiovascular devices, neuromodulation, structural heart, and diabetic care. Margins are 35–40%; growth is driven by aging populations and emerging market procedure adoption.

The critical metric for dividend and total return growth is the degree to which Diagnostics and Devices growth can offset Established Pharma's decline and support organic growth of 4–6% annually. Capital intensity is moderate; the company spends 4–5% of revenue on R&D and maintains a disciplined M&A strategy focused on tuck-in acquisitions and selective bolt-on deals in high-growth verticals.

## Competitive Moat

Abbott's moat is multifaceted: (1) **Scale and Distribution** — Abbott controls 40%+ market share in point-of-care diagnostics globally; the installed base of healthcare systems using Abbott instruments creates high switching costs. Distribution advantages enable rapid new product rollouts and market penetration. (2) **Brands and Market Presence** — Similac is the #1 infant formula brand in North America and globally; Ensure owns significant share in adult nutrition. Brand equity translates to pricing power and customer loyalty. (3) **Recurring Revenue Models** — Diagnostics and Nutrition generate recurring consumables and procedure revenue, creating predictable cash flows. (4) **Regulatory Barriers** — FDA approvals for diagnostics and devices create multi-year development cycles and high barriers to entry for competitors.

However, the moat is under pressure in specific areas. Diagnostics competition is intensifying: Roche, Bio-Rad, and others are contesting installed-base switchers with superior automation and throughput capabilities. Infant formula is facing biosimilar/private-label competition in some geographies, and regulatory scrutiny (e.g., FDA formula shortages) has accelerated generic entrants. Medical devices face competition from Boston Scientific, Medtronic, and private equity-backed competitors. The company's ability to sustain moat depends on continued investment in innovation and maintaining regulatory/reimbursement advantage.

The company's portfolio has limited true blockbuster drugs (AbbVie took the crown jewels in 2013), so near-term growth is dependent on device and diagnostic innovation. M&A integration track record is solid, but execution risk remains on larger bolt-on acquisitions.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Abbott is a defensible, dividend-growth compounder with exposure to secular healthcare tailwinds: aging populations (diagnostics, devices), premature infants in emerging markets (nutrition), and chronic disease prevalence (devices). Diagnostics and Devices should drive 5–8% organic growth, supported by stable 35–40% gross margins and disciplined capital allocation (60–70% payout ratio). Dividend growth potential of 7–10% annually is supported by mid-single-digit organic growth and modest inorganic tuck-ins. Valuation at 14–17x forward P/E reflects reasonable risk-adjusted return potential for dividend and total-return investors. Balance sheet is investment-grade and provides capacity for M&A and shareholder returns.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** (1) **Diagnostics Competition** — If Roche or other competitors gain traction with installed-base switchers or superior automation, Abbott's market share could compress and pricing power could deteriorate. Each 100 basis points of market share loss reduces diagnostic segment revenue by $80M+. (2) **Infant Formula Margin Compression** — Regulatory pressure, increased competition, and emerging market pricing pressure could compress Similac margins faster than expected; private-label formula is gaining share. (3) **Device Reimbursement Headwinds** — Medicare rate setting and hospital GPO consolidation could pressure device pricing and volume growth below consensus expectations. (4) **M&A Execution Risk** — Recent and future acquisitions could underperform expectations or face integration challenges, impairing ROI on capital deployed.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Diagnostics segment organic growth rate and market share trends vs. Roche, Bio-Rad, and others; watch for installed-base switcher activity
- Nutrition revenue trends by region (North America, emerging markets) and margin trajectory; model pricing power and private-label competition
- Medical Devices growth by therapeutic area (cardiovascular, neuro, structural heart); track emerging market adoption and reimbursement trends
- Established Pharma decline rate and remaining patent cliff exposure; model impact on consolidated growth rates
- M&A pipeline and acquisition valuation metrics; assess whether recent deals are generating expected synergies and revenue accretion

## Valuation Archetype: Defensive Dividend Growth Compounder

**Primary Metrics to Track:**
- **Organic Revenue Growth Rate** — Monitor by segment; target is 5–7% consolidated, with Diagnostics and Devices at 5–8%, Nutrition at 6–9%, Pharma declining 3–5%
- **Gross Margin Evolution** — Track by segment; watch for compression in Nutrition (formula competition) or Diagnostics (competitive pricing); target is 38–42% consolidated
- **Free Cash Flow & Payout Ratio** — Track FCF conversion and dividend + buyback payout; target is 60–70% of FCF, supporting 7–10% dividend growth
- **Diagnostic Installed Base & Market Share** — Monitor point-of-care market share and switcher activity; key metric is competitive positioning vs. Roche in laboratory testing
- **Nutrition and Emerging Market Penetration** — Track Similac and Ensure revenue in emerging markets; model contribution to long-term growth
