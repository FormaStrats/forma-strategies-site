## Business Overview

Chevron is a fully integrated energy company with operations spanning upstream (crude oil and natural gas production), downstream (refining and fuels distribution), and specialty chemicals. The company operates a geographically diversified asset base across North America, South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, and Australia. Chevron's competitive moat rests on its scale, capital discipline, and position in advantaged and low-cost basins—particularly the Permian Basin in the U.S. and emerging operations in the deepwater Gulf of Mexico. The company competes with major integrated peers (ExxonMobil, Shell, TotalEnergies) by emphasizing returns-focused capital allocation and long-cycle project execution.

Chevron's revenue architecture is approximately 60-65% upstream (oil and liquids production) and 35-40% downstream (refining, retail, and chemicals). Upstream production is anchored by the Permian Basin (accounting for roughly 25% of company-wide production), supplemented by significant international legacy assets and emerging deepwater projects. The 2026 guidance implies 7-10% production growth, reflecting Permian ramp-up and Gulf of Mexico developments. Downstream profitability is highly dependent on realized refining margins, which compress during crude gluts and expand when demand outpaces supply. The company targets low oil-price breakeven (sub-$50 Brent for operational profitability at mature assets), providing substantial leverage to commodity price recovery.

Chevron is executing a deliberate energy transition strategy: grow oil and gas production in the lower-cost segments while investing modestly in lower-carbon energy and hydrogen. This contrasts with European majors' aggressive renewables pivots. The company divested certain legacy assets and is using capital returns and selective M&A to balance growth investment with shareholder returns. Recent strategic focus includes maximizing the value of Permian acreage, accelerating Gulf of Mexico developments (Anchor, Tahiti), and small-scale hydrogen pilot programs. Execution risk centers on subsea project delivery timelines and commodity price dependency.

## Revenue Architecture

Chevron derives roughly two-thirds of cash flow from upstream operations, where production growth and realized prices dominate economics. The Permian Basin is the crown jewel, with low all-in sustaining costs (sub-$30/BOE) enabling returns even at $40-50 Brent. Deepwater Gulf of Mexico projects (Anchor, Midale, Tahiti expansions) carry higher exploration risk but offer 25-30 year reserve lives and returns >12% at mid-cycle prices. International legacy operations (West Africa, Southeast Asia) generate stable base production but face resource nationalism and depletion.

Downstream operations (refining, aviation fuel, chemicals, Chevron Phillips partnership) contribute 30-35% of EBITDA with significantly lower cyclicality than upstream. Refining margins (margin over crude cost) ranged 8-14% over the 2023-2025 period, with the company maintaining 1.8M barrels/day of global refining capacity. This segment provides margin diversification when crude prices collapse. Capital allocation targets 40-50% dividend payout ratios, modest buybacks, and reinvestment in high-return projects. Net capex guidance typically ranges $13-15B annually, concentrated in Permian and Gulf deepwater over the medium term.

## Competitive Moat

Chevron's competitive advantages derive from three sources: (1) low-cost production from Permian scale and mature assets, providing cost leadership when commodity prices decline; (2) integrated downstream operations that capture midstream economics and provide hedges during crude oversupply; and (3) balance sheet strength (investment-grade ratings, FCF generation >$10B annually at $60 Brent) enabling patient capital deployment through commodity cycles.

The Permian position is particularly defensible: the company holds roughly 6-7M net acres in the core Midland Basin with resource density of 6-10 BOE/acre, competing favorably against peer positions. Capital efficiency per incremental barrel is below $25/BOE, placing Chevron in the cost leader quintile. Risk concentration emerges if crude prices fall into the $40-50 range sustained for multiple years, where even lower-cost assets see returns erode; a sustained macro slowdown could suppress demand, offsetting OPEC+ discipline.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Permian growth trajectory extends production upside through 2027-2028 at rates approaching 1M BOE/day company-wide; disciplined capital allocation and flat balance sheet will support consistent 5-8% dividend growth plus opportunistic buybacks, driving mid-single-digit total returns. A reversion to $70-75 Brent in a cyclical recovery scenario yields 20%+ FCF yield and justifies current valuation multiples.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** Energy transition accelerates faster than priced (policy, consumer demand, EV adoption), suppressing long-term crude demand and stranding Permian reserve value; sustained crude prices below $55 create annual dividend coverage pressure and force project deferrals. Deepwater project delays (common in subsea execution) push production ramps right and increase capital requirements, eroding FCF conversion.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Permian unit economics and well productivity trends YoY; validate 7-10% guidance assumptions
- Realized oil price realization vs. Brent spot; track hedging ratios and price realizations
- Gulf of Mexico project schedules and cost inflation; Anchor ramp timing critical for 2026-27 growth
- Refining margin environment and feedstock slate optimization; track chemical integration profitability
