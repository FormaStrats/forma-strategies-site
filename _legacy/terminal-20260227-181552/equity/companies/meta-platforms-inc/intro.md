## Business Overview

Meta Platforms Inc. operates the world's largest social media network, with ~3.2B monthly active users across its "Family" of apps: Facebook (3B users, 65-70% daily active), Instagram (2B users, skewing younger and visual-commerce focused), WhatsApp (2B users, encrypted messaging with emerging monetization), and Messenger (931M users, integrated with Facebook). The company generates 98%+ of revenue through advertising, leveraging first-party user data (demographic, behavioral, purchase signals) to enable precise targeting for advertisers across feed (News Feed, Reels), Stories, and Marketplace. Meta's advertising technology stack is purpose-built for performance marketing: audience segmentation (demographic, lookalike, custom audiences), real-time bidding, and proprietary machine learning algorithms for conversion prediction and cost optimization create a formidable competitive moat.

Meta's strategic inflection under CEO Mark Zuckerberg, post-2021, has centered on three pillars: (1) Efficiency obsession ("Year of Efficiency," 2023-2025)—the company cut $37B in annual operating expenses and reduced headcount 25%, improving operating margin from 12% to 40% while maintaining revenue growth; (2) Reels monetization—Meta has migrated 35-45% of daily active user time from Feed (higher RPM, $45-65 CPM) to Reels (lower RPM, $15-25 CPM), creating a structural headwind on revenue growth but improving long-term positioning vs. TikTok; (3) AI infrastructure investment—Meta has invested $20B+ in data center capex and is positioning as a primary provider of open-source LLMs (Llama models) and AI-powered ad targeting, creating optionality for incremental monetization via Llama licensing and enterprise AI services.

Meta's business model is fundamentally different from peers: the company has no significant non-advertising revenue, no subscription products with material scale (Instagram+ and Facebook+ have <10M paid users), and limited e-commerce monetization outside Marketplace. This creates concentration risk but also operational simplicity. Capex intensity has increased from 8-10% of revenue (pre-2023) to 18-20% currently as Meta builds GPU infrastructure for AI training and inference, signaling potential margin pressure in 2026-2027. Capital returns have accelerated: the company authorized $120B in buybacks and returned $80B+ in 2024-2025, supporting double-digit EPS CAGR despite revenue growth moderating.

## Revenue Architecture

Meta's unit economics are among the best in the industry. Cost-per-click (CPC) for Facebook/Instagram advertising averages $0.20-0.50 with advertiser conversion rates of 1-3%, yielding customer acquisition cost (CAC) of $1-2 per $10 customer lifetime value (LTV). This 10:1 LTV/CAC ratio compares favorably to peers and explains advertising ROI resiliency even during cyclical downturns. Revenue per daily active user (ARPU) has grown 25-35% from FY2020-FY2025 through a combination of (1) geographic mix shift toward higher-monetizing markets (North America, Europe), (2) expansion of advertising inventory (Marketplace ads, Reels pre-roll, Messenger ads), (3) average revenue per user (ARPU) growth within existing geographies (20%+ YoY in mature markets).

Reels monetization represents the primary near-term headwind and strategic inflection. Meta's Reels inventory has grown from <5% of user time in 2020 to 35-45% currently, creating a 20-30% ARPU drag because Reels CPM ($15-25) is 40-50% lower than Feed CPM ($45-65) due to lower advertiser intent (users browsing entertainment vs. actively shopping). However, management believes Reels monetization will improve 60-100% by 2027 through: (1) AI-powered feed/Reels insertion (showing ads at moments of highest engagement), (2) video commerce integration (shoppable Reels), (3) Reels-specific advertiser tools and formats. If Reels RPM reaches $30-35 CPM by 2027, ARPU growth could accelerate to 12-15% annually vs. 5-8% currently.

Geographic ARPU dispersion is material: North America ARPU is $200+ per DAU annually, Europe is $100-120 per DAU annually, but Asia-Pacific (India, Indonesia, Philippines) is $5-10 per DAU annually. Meta's 3B user base is 60% concentrated in lower-monetizing regions, creating headwind on blended ARPU growth. However, this also represents TAM expansion: if India ARPU reaches $25-30 per DAU by 2028 (still 10x below North America), incremental revenue opportunity exceeds $15B annually.

## Competitive Moat

Meta's moat is built on scale network effects and advertising technology: more users attract more advertisers, more advertisers create better-targeted experiences, better experiences attract more users. This flywheel is self-reinforcing. The company's 3.2B user base and 60 years of aggregated behavioral data creates a targeting advantage that is difficult to replicate. Facebook's original business case (university social network) has matured, but Instagram remains the primary growth vector for Millennials and Gen-Z audiences, creating defensibility vs. emerging competitors.

However, the moat is under structural pressure from three vectors. First, TikTok's superior recommendation algorithm (ByteDance's For You Page) has demonstrated superior ability to surface compelling short-form video content, resulting in TikTok capturing 17% of US digital ad spend as advertisers follow their user engagement. Second, messaging apps (Signal, Telegram) are increasingly dominant for private communication, reducing Meta's stickiness in the messaging category. Third, regulatory pressure (EU's Digital Markets Act, potential US regulation) is eroding Meta's ability to leverage cross-app data for targeting, likely compressing advertiser ROI and reducing pricing power.

Instagram Reels has been positioned as Meta's TikTok competitor, but early adoption data shows Meta is 18-24 months behind TikTok in recommendation algorithm sophistication. The company is investing heavily to close this gap through AI/ML, but the risk of permanent share loss remains meaningful. Meta's e-commerce ambitions (Facebook Marketplace, Instagram Shopping) have underperformed relative to expectations, and Amazon's dominance in high-intent commerce remains uncontested.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Reels monetization inflection will unlock 15-20% ARPU growth by 2027 as Reels CPM reaches $30-35 per impression and video commerce scales. Meta's investment in Llama open-source LLMs and AI-powered ad targeting will enable 5-10% incremental revenue uplift through improved advertiser ROI and lower CAC. International user monetization (particularly India, Southeast Asia) will grow 25-30% annually as smartphone penetration deepens and ARPU increases to $15-25 per DAU. Operating margin will sustain 40-45% through disciplined capex efficiency, with capex intensity declining to 12-14% of revenue by 2028. The company will return $100B+ annually via buybacks, supporting 18-22% EPS CAGR through 2027. At 24x NTM earnings and 0.3% dividend yield, the valuation is reasonable for a 15% perpetual growth story with 40%+ ROIC.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** Reels monetization is proceeding slower than expected; CPM may plateau at $20-25 vs. management's $35-40 target, limiting ARPU growth to 8-10% and missing revenue guidance. TikTok's superior algorithm is capturing disproportionate user engagement (17% of digital ad spend with <3 years presence), suggesting permanent share loss to Meta; bans or restrictions on TikTok in the US (2026 possibility) may be net-positive for Meta, but full erasure of threat is unlikely. Regulatory headwinds in EU (data usage restrictions, forced divestiture scenarios) could reduce Meta's ability to leverage first-party data for targeting, compressing advertiser ROI and CPMs by 20-30%. Capex intensity is rising (18-20% of revenue currently) as Meta builds AI infrastructure; if GPU costs don't decline and utilization is lower than expected, margins could compress 200-300bps by 2027, offsetting operational leverage gains. Advertiser ROI metrics are deteriorating: cost-per-acquisition (CPA) for advertisers has increased 15-20% YoY while conversion rates have declined, suggesting ROI compression and potential budget reallocation to higher-performing channels (Google, TikTok).

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Reels ARPU and RPM trends by geography and format; quantify path to $30-35 CPM realization
- International ARPU growth trajectory, particularly India; model time to $15-20 ARPU per DAU
- Advertiser ROI metrics and cost-per-acquisition trends; assess if deterioration is temporary or structural
- Capex intensity trajectory and GPU utilization rates; model impact of data center investments on margin expansion
- Competitive dynamics vs. TikTok: engagement metrics, creator loyalty, advertiser spend allocation by platform
