## Business Overview

Merck & Co. is a diversified biopharmaceutical company centered on three therapeutic areas: Oncology (40%+ of revenue), Infectious Diseases/Vaccines (30%+), and General Medicine/Other (25%+). The company's investment thesis is predicated on the performance of a single franchise: Keytruda (pembrolizumab, PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor), which has become the industry's dominant oncology asset and is projected to generate peak annual revenues of $15–20B globally before patent expiration in 2028 (U.S.) and 2029–2030 (Europe). Keytruda has been approved across multiple solid tumors (lung, melanoma, gastric, head-and-neck, renal, colorectal) and hematologic malignancies, providing diversified revenue streams within the franchise.

The strategic inflection is stark: Keytruda accounts for approximately 40% of Merck's revenue (~$20–22B annually by 2024–2025) and represents an estimated 50%+ of operating profit. The patent cliff is imminent—LOE in 2028 (U.S.) will be a significant EPS headwind. Merck has aggressively pursued in-licensing and M&A to build a pipeline of next-generation oncology assets intended to replace Keytruda revenue post-LOE. Recent acquisitions include Prometheus Biopharmaceuticals, Hoth Therapeutics, and numerous in-licensing deals for checkpoint inhibitor combinations and novel mechanisms. The company is betting that immunotherapy + combination approaches (Keytruda + chemotherapy, Keytruda + targeted therapies, Keytruda + novel IO agents) will sustain oncology franchise growth even as monotherapy Keytruda declines.

The infectious disease and vaccine portfolio (including gardasil HPV vaccine, pneumococcal vaccines, Propecia in dermatology) is stable and profitable but not a growth driver. The company faces significant patent cliff risk: several franchises will lose exclusivity in 2026–2028, creating a compressed time window for the pipeline to de-risk before revenue erosion accelerates. Execution on the pipeline is binary; if late-stage oncology programs fail or face competitive losses, Merck's growth trajectory resets.

## Revenue Architecture

Merck's revenue is increasingly dependent on Keytruda's sustained dominance in the oncology market. Keytruda represents 40–45% of total pharmaceutical revenue and generates gross margins of 70–75% (premium oncology pricing). The franchise benefits from label expansion (new indications, earlier lines of therapy, monotherapy vs. combination) and emerging market penetration. However, competitive pressure from Roche (Tecentriq), AstraZeneca (Imfinzi), and others is intensifying; Merck's market share in certain oncology niches is being contested by competitors with combination approaches or superior tolerability profiles.

Revenue from vaccines and infectious diseases (Gardasil, pneumococcal vaccines, Rotateq) is $3–4B annually with mid-single-digit growth driven by expanded global vaccination programs and booster campaigns. Legacy assets in general medicine are declining from patent erosion. The critical metric is the degree to which Merck's oncology pipeline can offset Keytruda's decline post-patent cliff. Current guidance assumes flat-to-modest growth through 2025–2026, with accelerating decline thereafter absent significant pipeline contribution.

R&D intensity is 22–25% of revenue, reflecting aggressive investment in late-stage oncology programs. Capital allocation is disciplined: the company maintains investment-grade debt and targets a dividend payout ratio of 40–45%, leaving capacity for inorganic growth and modest share repurchases.

## Competitive Moat

Merck's moat in oncology rests on Keytruda's clinical dominance and physician familiarity. The PD-1 pathway is now crowded with competitors (Roche's Tecentriq, AstraZeneca's Imfinzi, Bristol-Myers Squibb's Opdivo, others), but Keytruda maintains pricing power and market share due to broad approval across multiple indications and strong clinical trial performance. The installed base of patients and oncologists trained on Keytruda-based regimens provides some inertia.

However, the moat is eroding. Newer mechanisms (PD-L1 + LAG3 combinations, TIM-3 inhibitors, bispecific antibodies) are demonstrating superior efficacy in certain settings, fragmenting the IO market. Combination approaches (checkpoint inhibitor + chemotherapy, checkpoint inhibitor + targeted therapy) are becoming standard-of-care, reducing monotherapy Keytruda's relevance. Biosimilar PD-1 inhibitors could launch pre-patent expiration, accelerating price erosion. The long-term moat depends entirely on Merck's pipeline success in next-generation IO mechanisms and combination strategies.

Vaccine revenue benefits from high switching costs (healthcare systems prefer stable suppliers) and regulatory barriers (approval timelines are lengthy), but this is a mature, low-growth business and not strategic to the investment narrative. The company's past M&A integration record (e.g., Schering-Plough acquisition in 2009) has been solid, but recent smaller bolt-on acquisitions have had mixed results in terms of pipeline advancement and revenue realization.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Merck is a post-LOE pharma positioned to survive patent cliff through disciplined pipeline development and strategic M&A. Keytruda's dominance in oncology is durable through 2028–2030 despite competitive pressure; the franchise is broadening into earlier lines of therapy (adjuvant, neoadjuvant) and combination approaches that sustain revenue. If late-stage IO pipeline programs advance to approval, Merck could establish a next-generation franchise offsetting Keytruda's decline. The vaccine portfolio (Gardasil, pneumococcal) provides stable, recurring revenue. Dividend and share repurchases should support 4–6% EPS growth through 2027, with accelerating pressure thereafter. Valuation at 12–15x forward P/E reflects reasonable risk-adjusted return potential if pipeline execution de-risks.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** (1) **Keytruda Patent Cliff** — Faster-than-expected LOE (via biosimilar launch pre-2028 or accelerated generic pathway) would trigger significant revenue decline; each year of LOE acceleration reduces EPS by 5–10% at the trough. (2) **Pipeline Attrition** — Late-stage oncology programs (Phase III) could fail or face unexpected safety signals; a major pipeline setback would reset near-term growth expectations and force cost cutting. (3) **Competitive Intensity** — Roche, AstraZeneca, and others are aggressively pursuing IO + combination therapies; Merck's pipeline programs face crowded indication spaces with binary regulatory risk. (4) **Acquisition Execution Risk** — Recent M&A and in-licensing deals carry integration and development risk; if acquired assets fail to deliver, Merck faces wasted capital and opportunity costs.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Keytruda revenue trend by indication and geography; model market share trajectory vs. competitors and estimate LOE impact across geographies
- Late-stage oncology pipeline programs (Phase III readouts) with focus on efficacy, safety, and regulatory pathway clarity; this is the de-risking variable
- In-licensed asset progress and development timelines; assess probability of clinical success and peak sales estimates for key programs
- Vaccine franchise stability (Gardasil, pneumococcal) and emerging market penetration; this provides downside support but is not a growth driver
- M&A integration progress and ROI on recent acquisitions; track whether acquired R&D productivity translates to pipeline advancement
