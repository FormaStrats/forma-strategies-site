## Business Overview

Amazon.com Inc. is a diversified platform conglomerate organized into three reporting segments: North America (retail, advertising, third-party seller services), International (retail and seller services outside US), and Amazon Web Services (cloud infrastructure, databases, analytics). The company has evolved from an online retailer into a three-pillar business where AWS (representing ~18% of revenue) generates 30-35% operating margins, advertising (5-6% of revenue) operates at 50%+ margins, while retail (55-60% of revenue) operates at 3-5% margins. This portfolio structure creates a powerful financial machine: profitability is increasingly driven by high-margin cloud and advertising, allowing retail segment to operate with minimal margins while using logistics scale and customer engagement as moats.

Amazon's retail ecosystem spans consumer-to-consumer marketplaces (third-party sellers representing 60%+ of GMV), first-party inventory, fulfillment through 400M+ square feet of warehouse and logistics infrastructure, and fintech services (Amazon Pay, Amazon Credit). Prime membership (200M+ global subscribers) is the cornerstone product: members pay $139-180 annually for fast shipping, streaming video, music, and reading content, creating a 90%+ retention rate and embedded purchasing acceleration. AWS is the gold standard in cloud infrastructure—70% market share in specialized workloads like data analytics, machine learning, and containerized applications—and is strategically critical as Amazon's primary profit engine and cash generator. Advertising (display ads, sponsored search, video ads) is the fastest-growing segment, leveraging retail customer data and shopping intent signal to monetize product listings and create a $50B+ TAM.

Amazon's strategic inflection under CEO Andy Jassy (since 2021) has centered on three pillars: (1) profitability prioritization—the company shifted from revenue growth at all costs to operating margin expansion, evidenced by 30-35% EBIT margin target by 2027 vs. 10-15% historically; (2) AI infrastructure leadership—the company is positioning AWS as the primary provider of GPU capacity and foundational AI services (Amazon Bedrock, SageMaker), betting on long-term AI workload durability; (3) advertising expansion—the company is building a first-party data asset (retail shopping behavior) to compete with Google and Meta on targeting precision. Capital allocation remains conservative: minimal dividends, minimal buybacks, with free cash flow reinvested into logistics, data centers, and R&D. The company generated $80B+ in free cash flow in 2025 and is reinvesting 60%+ back into the business.

## Revenue Architecture

Amazon operates three distinct economic models. Retail segment (North America and International combined) generates 55-60% of revenue at 40-42% gross margins but 3-5% operating margins after absorbing logistics, fulfillment, and customer service costs. The retail moat is primarily convenience and selection (100M+ SKUs) rather than price leadership; Prime membership drives customer lock-in and increases customer lifetime value through accelerated repurchase and category expansion. Third-party seller services (FBA, advertising, fulfillment) represent 20%+ of retail revenue at 70%+ gross margins, creating a virtuous cycle where sellers benefit from traffic while Amazon captures incremental margin.

AWS generates 18% of revenue at 90%+ gross margins and 35-40% operating margins. Unit economics are exceptional: enterprise customers typically start with a small project ($50K-100K annually), then compound to $500K-1M+ as they migrate additional workloads. AWS pricing has declined 10-15% annually through reserved instance discounts and competitive pressure, yet revenue per customer has increased 20%+ through workload deepening. The company's dominant market position in infrastructure, databases (DynamoDB, RDS), analytics (Redshift, Athena), and emerging AI services (Bedrock, SageMaker) creates moat defensibility: switching costs are substantial, and retraining engineering teams on new infrastructure represents 6-12 month commitments.

Advertising represents ~5-6% of revenue but is growing 20%+ annually and operates at 50-60% gross margins. The business model leverages first-party retail data (what customers search, view, buy) to enable precise targeting for advertisers. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) for new advertisers is minimal because advertising platform sits within a transaction marketplace that already serves advertiser needs. The key lever is expanding from product-listing ads to video, display, and sponsored search outside of Amazon.com, creating a broader ad network.

## Competitive Moat

Amazon's moat is multifaceted and self-reinforcing. The retail moat is anchored in logistics and selection: the company's 400M+ square feet of fulfillment infrastructure and 50-day average inventory turnover enable 1-2 day delivery at scale and minimal stockouts. This creates a flywheel: fast delivery attracts more customers → more customers justify more distribution centers → denser network enables faster delivery. The Prime ecosystem (membership, shipping, video, music, reading, payments) creates switching costs measured in hours of adoption friction and habit. Network effects are real but limited to third-party sellers, not consumers (Walmart and Target have similarly efficient logistics, but Walmart's selection lag remains a disadvantage).

AWS moat is fortress-grade and hardening. The company has 8+ years of head start vs. Azure and GCP, resulting in (1) broadest service portfolio (200+ services vs. 100+ for Azure/GCP), (2) largest trained customer base and ecosystem of system integrators, (3) deepest network of availability zones and geographic redundancy, (4) lowest-cost infrastructure through scale volume discounts. Switching costs are substantial: re-architecting a 500-engineer team's infrastructure to GCP or Azure typically requires 12-24 months. The competitive response from Microsoft (bundling Azure + Microsoft 365 + Dynamics) is real and gaining traction in enterprise accounts, but AWS remains the category leader with 32% market share.

Advertising moat is emerging but not yet fortress-grade. First-party retail data (what customers search and buy on Amazon) is a material advantage vs. Google and Meta, but advertisers have increasingly shifted ad spend to marketplaces with broader reach. The key question is whether Amazon can expand advertising beyond Amazon.com (e.g., to external publishers) at the scale and ROI that advertisers expect. Currently, advertising outside Amazon.com represents <10% of advertising revenue.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** AWS will sustain 18-22% revenue growth through 2027 as AI/ML workload migration accelerates, with gross margin expansion to 92-94% through infrastructure efficiency gains and higher-margin AI services mix. Advertising revenue will compound 25%+ annually to become 12-15% of total revenue by 2028, with 50%+ margins providing operating leverage. Retail operating margin will expand 50-100bps annually through fulfillment automation, logistics density optimization, and mix-shift toward higher-margin third-party services. The company will reach 30-35% consolidated operating margin by 2027 vs. 15-17% currently, unlocking $50-75B in incremental annual operating profit and driving 20%+ free cash flow growth. At 32x NTM earnings and 1.8% implied FCF yield, the valuation is reasonable for a 15% perpetual growth story with 35%+ ROIC and $100B+ annual free cash flow.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** AWS growth is decelerating (22% YoY in recent quarters vs. 25%+ two years ago) due to customer capex rationalization and competitive pricing pressure from Azure; growth may moderate to 12-15% by 2027, below market expectations. Advertising monetization is hitting headwinds: advertisers face diminishing returns from sponsored search and are shifting budgets to social and video; Amazon's advertising growth may decelerate below 20% if advertiser benchmarking reveals ROI compression. Retail margin expansion is constrained by wage inflation in logistics (Amazon's average fulfillment center wage of $20-22 per hour is up 20% from 2019), commodity shipping costs, and competitive pricing pressure from Walmart+; operating margin expansion may plateau at 10-12% vs. the 30%+ target. Antitrust action against Amazon's marketplace (particularly concerning self-preferencing practices) could impose structural costs through operational separation or commission rate limits, directly impacting profitability. The company is trading at 32x earnings, a 50% premium to Azure/GCP due to AWS moat perception, leaving limited margin of safety if growth disappoints.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- AWS revenue growth trajectory by customer segment (enterprise vs. SMB) and workload type (AI/ML vs. traditional infrastructure); verify 18-22% growth sustainability
- AWS gross margin progression; quantify impact of chip cost inflation (GPUs, custom silicon) on unit economics
- Advertising revenue growth rate and advertiser concentration; assess if ROI metrics are plateauing for sponsored search
- Retail operating margin trajectory; model wage inflation impact on fulfillment center unit economics
- Amazon's market share in AI/ML workloads and positioning vs. Azure (enterprise bundling advantage) and GCP (data analytics advantage)
