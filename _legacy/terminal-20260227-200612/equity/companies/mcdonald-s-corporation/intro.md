## Business Overview

McDonald's is the world's largest quick-service restaurant operator, boasting 41,000+ locations across 100+ countries and generating 95%+ of revenue from franchisee royalties and rent. The company operates a capital-light franchise-licensor model: franchisees own ~87% of locations, bear 100% of unit-level capex and labor costs, and pay McDonald's 5–6% of revenue as base royalties plus occupancy rent (where McDonald's owns the real estate). This structure defers customer-facing operational risk to franchisees while centralizing profit extraction through royalty mechanisms and real estate ownership—a deliberate design that yields 25%+ operating margins despite the company being essentially a real estate and IP licensing firm.

Geographic exposure splits across US (35% revenue, 50%+ operating margins), International Operated Markets (Europe, APAC, Japan; 27% revenue, 35–40% margins), and International Developmental Licensed Markets (emerging markets; 11% revenue, 30–35% margins). Corporate operates 6–8% of locations (US primarily), capturing company-operated restaurant revenue and G&A expenses. The company generates $22–24B in annual revenue, 60–65% of which flows to operating income before corporate overhead and investment income.

Strategic direction centers on three vectors: (1) **Franchisee economics optimization**—raising royalty rates and rent from selective franchisees, improving unit economics for company-operated stores through automation/labor reduction, (2) **Digital/Delivery Growth**—incorporating mobile ordering, loyalty programs, and third-party delivery partnerships to drive traffic and frequency, (3) **Emerging-market penetration**—capex discipline in franchise deployment to high-growth geographies (India, Brazil, China) where company-owned real estate can capture appreciation and ensure long-term control.

## Revenue Architecture

McDonald's three-tiered revenue waterfall reveals margin concentration: (1) **Franchise Royalties** (~70% of revenue)—corporate collects 5–6% of franchisee revenue with near-100% contribution margin, generating intrinsic profit per dollar of franchisee sales. (2) **Occupancy Rent** (~15% of revenue)—real estate owned by McDonald's (45–50% of locations) generates rent based on percentage of franchisee revenue or fixed lease payments; 70%+ contribution margin given fully depreciated real estate. (3) **Company-Operated Restaurant Revenue** (~10–12% of revenue)—minimal margins (5–8%) after COGS, labor, and occupancy; primarily deployed for R&D, market entry, and training.

The operational leverage is extraordinary: a $1M increase in franchisee sales yields ~$60k royalty revenue with 95%+ incremental margin (minimal incremental cost). Same-store sales growth (positive comps) flows almost entirely to operating income. Conversely, negative comps compress royalty revenue with equal velocity. The key distinction between McDonald's and typical QSR operators: 90%+ of profit derives from licensing/real estate, not restaurant operations. This fundamentally changes cash flow modeling—capex is minimal ($1–2B annually, primarily for franchisee support and company-operated store maintenance), and FCF conversion is exceptional (~90% of operating income).

Digital initiatives (mobile, loyalty, delivery) are margin-accretive: loyalty members exhibit 2–3x higher visit frequency than non-members; delivery comps drive incremental traffic with near-zero incremental cost. The addressable opportunity: converting 20–30% of the customer base into loyalty members could yield +2–3% comp growth sustainably.

## Competitive Moat

McDonald's moat is behavioral/franchise-structural, not operational: (1) **Brand Equity**—iconic, globally recognized, and associated with value, convenience, and consistency. This permits pricing power; company has raised prices 5–8% YoY during 2022–2024 with <1% traffic loss, demonstrating inelastic demand. (2) **Franchisee Dependency**—the company owns real estate and has contractual control over menus, pricing, operations standards. Franchisees cannot easily switch operators (switching costs are embedded in the lease structure) nor can new entrants replicate the real estate portfolio and supplier relationships. (3) **Scale in Real Estate**—McDonald's owns/leases 45–50% of all locations globally; no competitor holds comparable real estate scale. This provides collateral for cheap capital and geographic flexibility. (4) **Supply Chain**—centralized procurement and supplier relationships yield input cost advantages that franchisees rely on; proprietary food specs (beef, chicken sourcing) create switching costs.

Competitive threats are measured: QSR consolidation (Yum!, Restaurant Brands, Dine Global) creates comparable scale, but no single competitor matches McDonald's global unit count or real estate control. Fast-casual (Chipotle, Panera) and delivery-native (DoorDash, Grubhub) platforms create category disruption but do not displace McDonald's core value proposition. Private-label/restaurant consolidation risk is low given franchisee diversity and McDonald's structural advantage.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** McDonald's generates $22–24B revenue from a quasi-real-estate monopoly. Pricing power demonstrated in 2022–2024 (5–8% annual price increases, minimal traffic loss) enables 4–5% organic growth even in flat-traffic environments. Emerging-market penetration in India, China, Brazil offers long-runway unit growth (5–10% annually for 10+ years). Digital/loyalty penetration (currently 20–30% of transactions) scaling to 50% could drive incremental 1–2% comp growth. Franchisee consolidation (smaller chains buying McDonald's locations) improves unit economics and franchisee credit quality. FCF generation $8–10B annually supports 50%+ payout ratios and accelerating buybacks (1–2% annual share reduction).

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** Macro recession compresses consumer visits to QSR; price elasticity increases if GDP contracts. Franchisee economic stress (rising labor costs, food inflation outpacing pricing flexibility) triggers rent/royalty pressure and potential franchisee failures in weak markets. Digital loyalty saturation and delivery economic exhaustion (margin compression from higher platform fees) stall comps inflection. Emerging-market geopolitical risks (India regulatory, China competition, Brazil macro) impair unit growth thesis. Labor activism and minimum-wage escalation (particularly US) compress franchisee margins and force rent concessions. Real estate market stress (commercial real estate devaluation) impairs collateral values and refinancing flexibility.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Same-store sales (SSS) growth by geography and sequencing (US, International, DLM) as indicator of pricing power maintenance
- Franchisee unit economics (royalty rate, rent coverage, EBITDA margins) and credit quality (default/bankruptcy trends)
- Digital/Loyalty penetration rates (% of transactions) and incremental traffic contribution (comp lift attribution)
- Real estate portfolio composition (owned vs. leased % by geography) and depreciation/amortization runway

## Valuation Archetype: Mature Franchise Licensor with Pricing Power

**Primary Metrics to Track:**
- Same-store sales (SSS) growth YoY (target: +2–4% sustainable; breakeven = volume stasis, >0% = pricing power)
- Organic revenue growth = SSS % + unit growth % (rule of thumb: 3–5% aggregate for mature market)
- Operating margin (% of revenue) YoY (benchmark: 40%+ for corporate segment; compression >100 bps signals franchisee stress)
- Franchisee average unit volume (AUV) and comps by segment (US, International, DLM; early indicator of pricing elasticity)
- Free Cash Flow / Operating Income ratio (target: 85–95% reflects efficient capex discipline and working capital management)
