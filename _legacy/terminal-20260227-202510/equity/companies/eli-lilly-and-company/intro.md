## Business Overview

Eli Lilly has undergone a dramatic transformation from a diversified pharma into a high-growth, precision-medicine-focused powerhouse anchored by a franchise that will reshape healthcare: the GLP-1 receptor agonist class for obesity and metabolic disease. The company's central investment thesis is the massive market opportunity in obesity and type 2 diabetes created by Mounjaro (tirzepatide, dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist) and Zepbound (tirzepatide, obesity indication). Mounjaro launched in 2022 and has become one of the fastest-growing pharmaceutical franchises in history, generating estimated $5–7B in annual revenue by 2025 with double-digit growth runway extending to 2030+. Zepbound, the obesity-indicated version of the same molecule, is ramping rapidly from launch in late 2023 and represents an incremental $2–3B+ opportunity.

Lilly's competitive moat in the GLP-1 space stems from Mounjaro's mechanism: tirzepatide is the only approved dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, offering superior weight loss and glycemic control compared to pure GLP-1 agonists (Novo Nordisk's Ozempic/Wegovy, Roche's candidate). This dual mechanism provides meaningful clinical differentiation that payers and patients value; head-to-head trial data shows Mounjaro delivering 20%+ weight loss vs. 17% for semaglutide. The company has a multi-year exclusivity runway before biosimilar or generic GLP-1 agents commoditize the market.

Beyond GLP-1s, Lilly maintains a diversified pharma portfolio: oncology (Cyramza, Alimta, Verzenio in breast cancer), immunology (Baricitinib for rheumatoid arthritis), and diabetes (legacy insulin products declining). However, the portfolio is increasingly peripheral to the investment narrative; Mounjaro and Zepbound will dominate cash flow and growth expectations through 2028–2030. The strategic risk is binary: if biosimilar or novel competitor GLP-1s (from Roche, Viking, Viking Therapeutics, or others) erode Mounjaro's market share faster than management expects, the valuation multiple resets sharply.

## Revenue Architecture

Eli Lilly's revenue architecture is increasingly dominated by the obesity/diabetes franchise. Mounjaro revenues are growing at 40–50% CAGR (2023–2025) and will represent 25–30% of total pharma revenue by 2026–2027. Zepbound is an incremental opportunity, adding an estimated $2–4B in peak revenue by cannibalizing some Mounjaro sales but also expanding the addressable market to non-diabetic obese patients. The dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism offers superior efficacy and clinical outcomes, commanding premium pricing ($1,200–$1,500 per month) with strong payer support for both indications.

The margin profile on Mounjaro and Zepbound is excellent: specialty pharma biologics with limited generic competition, high manufacturing barriers, and strong pricing power. Manufacturing is capital-intensive but leverages Lilly's existing biologics infrastructure. The company is investing heavily in capacity expansion to meet demand; supply constraints are a key near-term limiting factor on growth. By 2027–2028, peak-year tirzepatide revenues could reach $10–15B globally if market penetration assumptions hold and supply constraints ease.

The remainder of the portfolio (oncology, immunology, legacy diabetes) represents stable, lower-growth revenue ($15–20B annually) with mid-single-digit growth. Verzenio (CDK4/6 inhibitor in breast cancer) is a $2–3B franchise with growth runway, but it is increasingly competitive. The portfolio is not a growth driver; value creation is almost entirely driven by the tirzepatide franchise. If GLP-1 market penetration or pricing disappoints, Lilly reverts to a mid-single-digit growth pharma at valuations far below current levels.

## Competitive Moat

Eli Lilly's moat in GLP-1s is intellectual property and first-mover advantage in the dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism. Patent protection on tirzepatide extends through the 2030s, providing a multi-year runway before biosimilar versions could launch. The superior clinical profile (dual mechanism offering 20%+ weight loss vs. 17% for GLP-1-only agents) creates customer switching friction and payer preference. Manufacturing complexity and capacity constraints also create temporary competitive advantages: Lilly controls significant tirzepatide manufacturing capacity, and ramping additional capacity is capital-intensive and time-consuming.

However, the moat faces material competitive threats. Novo Nordisk's Ozempic and Wegovy are entrenched in the market and benefit from established physician familiarity and insurance coverage; reimbursement barriers for Mounjaro could limit penetration. Roche is developing finerenone-based combination therapies and has launched a GLP-1 agonist (Mounjaro competitor) in some markets. More critically, the GLP-1 market itself is at risk from new entrants: Roche, Viking Therapeutics, Viking Pharma, and others are developing novel GLP-1s and combination therapies. If biosimilar GLP-1s launch before expected (2030+), the competitive intensity and pricing could deteriorate faster than modeled.

The long-term moat depends on Lilly's ability to: (1) maintain Mounjaro's clinical/pricing advantage through Phase III and post-market data; (2) extend the franchise with combination therapies (tirzepatide + other agents) that provide incremental benefit; (3) achieve favorable reimbursement in key markets globally; and (4) manage supply/demand dynamics without creating persistent shortages that drive customers to competitors.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Eli Lilly is a rare pharma mega-cap with structural exposure to the obesity pandemic, which affects 40%+ of the global adult population. Mounjaro's dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism is clinically superior, supporting premium pricing and market share gains vs. pure GLP-1 competitors. Peak tirzepatide franchise revenue (Mounjaro + Zepbound) could reach $10–15B globally by 2028–2030, representing 25–30% of company revenue and 40%+ of operating profit. This growth trajectory supports 12–18% EPS growth through 2028, justifying a pharma sector premium multiple. The company's balance sheet is investment-grade, enabling both capital returns and continued inorganic growth in oncology and diabetes.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** (1) **Biosimilar Acceleration Risk** — If biosimilar GLP-1 pathways are expedited (FDA guidance could shift post-2027), competitive pricing pressure emerges sooner than model assumes, eroding tirzepatide's economics. (2) **Market Saturation** — Obesity market penetration may plateau below expectations if payer coverage becomes restrictive or patient compliance declines; GDP per capita in emerging markets could limit addressable market expansion. (3) **Competitive Encroachment** — Roche, Viking, and others launching competing GLP-1s or novel anti-obesity agents could fragment market share; Novo Nordisk's Ozempic incumbency is difficult to dislodge. (4) **Supply Constraints** — Persistent manufacturing capacity limitations could leave money on the table and drive customers to competitors; capital intensity of ramping may exceed expectations.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Mounjaro revenue growth YoY and market share within GLP-1 and broader anti-obesity market; model peak market penetration and pricing sustainability
- Zepbound ramping trajectory and cannibalization of Mounjaro; assess addressable market in non-diabetic obesity patients
- Biosimilar and novel competitor launch timelines; monitor regulatory filings and clinical trial readouts from Roche, Viking, and others
- Manufacturing capacity and supply constraints; assess capital deployment to meet near-term demand and long-term expansion needs
- Reimbursement dynamics by geography (U.S., Europe, Japan, emerging markets); watch for formulary restrictions or MLR increases in managed care

## Valuation Archetype: Growth Pharma with Blockbuster Franchise

**Primary Metrics to Track:**
- **Mounjaro + Zepbound Combined Revenue** — Track absolute revenue and YoY growth rate; target is 30–40% CAGR through 2027, then moderation to 15–20% as base effect sets in
- **Market Share vs. Novo Nordisk** — Monitor Mounjaro's share of GLP-1 and weight-loss medication market; key metric is whether Lilly gains share post-launch or maintains parity
- **Gross Margin on GLP-1s** — Track gross margin trajectory as manufacturing scales and mix shifts toward specialty pharma; target is 70–75% by 2027
- **Peak Sales Estimates by Indication** — Model Mounjaro peak ($7–10B) and Zepbound incremental peak ($2–4B); variance here cascades to valuation
- **Pipeline Advancement** — Monitor Phase III programs combining tirzepatide with other mechanisms (SGLT-2 inhibitors, GCG agonists, others); these could extend franchise longevity
