## Business Overview

Johnson & Johnson is in structural transition, executing a planned separation into two standalone healthcare companies: Innovative Medicine (pharmaceutical and biologics, remaining as J&J) and MedTech (medical devices and diagnostics, to be spun out). The separation, announced in late 2023 with targeted completion within 18–24 months (mid-2025 to mid-2026), marks the end of a 130-year-old diversified healthcare conglomerate and signals management's view that separate entities will be valued at a premium to the consolidated group. The investment thesis has bifurcated: J&J (Innovative Medicine post-separation) will be a pure-play pharma compounder with exposure to immunology, oncology, and cardiovascular therapeutics; the MedTech spinout will be a stable, recurring-revenue business in orthopedics, surgical instruments, and cardiovascular devices.

J&J Pharma's core portfolio rests on several enduring franchises, though key assets face patent cliffs. STELARA (ustekinumab, IL-12/IL-23 inhibitor for psoriasis, IBD, and rheumatoid arthritis) is the flagship immunology asset, having generated $10+ billion in peak annual revenue but now declining as Janssen's own Skyrizi (risankizumab, IL-23-selective, higher efficacy) cannibalizes share. The company is strategically sacrificing STELARA market share to promote Skyrizi—a classic internal competition scenario where the successor is superior. Imbruvica (ibrutinib, BTK inhibitor in chronic lymphocytic leukemia) is a $2B+ oncology asset facing biosimilar pressure. Durvalumab (PD-L1 inhibitor, Imjudo) is ramping in lung cancer but faces intense competition from Merck's Keytruda and others. The cardiology portfolio (Stelara, formerly used off-label for inflammation) includes legacy products, though newer mechanisms are in development.

The strategic inflection is clear: J&J is repositioning from a legacy-dependent portfolio toward newer immunology and precision oncology franchises, but execution risk is material. STELARA's accelerating decline, combined with Imbruvica erosion, creates a revenue headwind that must be offset by Skyrizi ramp and new launches. The MedTech spinout removes a valuable, stable cash-generative business, potentially depressing consolidated valuations and requiring the pharma entity to operate at higher leverage or reduced capital intensity. Near-term visibility is clouded by separation mechanics; medium-term visibility hinges on successful portfolio transition.

## Revenue Architecture

J&J's pharma revenue is generated across immunology (40%+), oncology (30%+), cardiovascular/other (30%), split pre-separation and post-separation between the Innovative Medicine entity and the MedTech spinout. Immunology is the primary battleground: STELARA declining from $10B+ peak to estimated $4–5B annually as Skyrizi ramps and biosimilars erode share. Skyrizi is the intended replacement, targeting $3–4B in annual revenue by 2027–2028 as market share stabilizes. However, Skyrizi faces intense competition from AbbVie's Skyrizi (same molecule, different manufacturer under licensing agreement—actually the same franchise) and emerging IL-23-selective inhibitors.

Oncology includes Imbruvica ($2–3B annually, declining due to biosimilar competition and alternative BTK inhibitors), Durvalumab (ramping toward $1–2B potential), Janssen's oncology pipeline (Phase II/III programs in various solid tumors), and legacy assets. The cardiology portfolio is mature and declining, with newer mechanisms in development to address CV risk in diabetes and hypertension. R&D is 12–15% of revenue; the company is investing in late-stage programs to fill pipeline gaps post-separation.

Margin profile post-separation will be higher for Innovative Medicine as a pure-play pharma, but with less stability from MedTech's recurring revenue. The company must navigate separation logistics (tax-efficient structure, debt allocation, IT separation) while executing product launches and managing patent cliffs. Near-term margin pressure is expected as separation costs and transition inefficiencies materialize; longer-term margins should expand as the pharma entity matures and consolidates.

## Competitive Moat

J&J's moat pre-separation was scale, distribution, and cross-selling capabilities across pharma and MedTech. Post-separation, the pharma entity's moat will depend on: (1) **Brand and Distribution Scale** — J&J benefits from established relationships with physicians, health systems, and payers; Janssen's heritage in immunology provides some switching friction. (2) **Patent Portfolio** — Key assets (Skyrizi, newer Janssen programs) have multi-year patent protection; however, the portfolio is less differentiated than competitors like Merck (Keytruda) or Lilly (Mounjaro). (3) **Manufacturing Capability** — J&J controls biologics manufacturing capacity, though this is not unique to the company.

The moat is vulnerable to erosion. STELARA is being cannibalized by internal competition (Skyrizi) and external pressure from AbbVie's Skyrizi and emerging IL-23 inhibitors. Imbruvica faces multiple BTK inhibitors (Calquence from AbbVie, Pirtobrutinib and others) and biosimilar pressure. The oncology portfolio is crowded with competitors (Merck's Keytruda dominance, Roche, AstraZeneca, others). The long-term moat depends on late-stage pipeline success in precision oncology and newer immunology mechanisms; if the pipeline stalls, J&J reverts to a declining portfolio of legacy assets.

Post-separation, the pharma entity loses the stabilizing benefit of MedTech's recurring revenue and must operate at higher leverage or reduced capital intensity. This could constrain inorganic growth and dividend growth compared to pre-separation expectations.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** J&J's separation unlocks value by allowing each entity to be valued on its own merits. The Innovative Medicine entity will be a pure-play pharma compounder with exposure to immunology and oncology secular tailwinds. Skyrizi can reach $3–4B in peak sales, partially offsetting STELARA's decline. New oncology launches (Durvalumab, pipeline programs) could drive high-single-digit revenue growth by 2027–2028. The separated entity will benefit from operating efficiency improvements and clearer strategic focus. Dividend and share buyback programs should support 5–8% EPS growth post-separation. The MedTech spinout will appeal to infrastructure and healthcare device investors, potentially trading at a premium multiple to the current conglomerate.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** (1) **STELARA Cliff** — If STELARA's decline accelerates faster than Skyrizi ramping (due to competitor Skyrizi cannibalization or emerging IL-23 inhibitors), the pharma entity faces significant revenue headwinds and requires aggressive pruning of costs or asset acquisitions. (2) **Separation Execution Risk** — The separation process is complex (IT separation, debt allocation, tax efficiency); execution failures could materialize integration and compliance costs that drag on near-term EPS. (3) **Leverage Post-Separation** — The pharma entity may operate at higher leverage post-MedTech spinout, constraining inorganic growth and increasing refinancing risk if interest rates remain elevated. (4) **Pipeline Binary Risk** — Late-stage oncology and immunology programs could fail Phase III trials, leaving the portfolio increasingly reliant on legacy assets with limited growth.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- STELARA sales decline rate by geography and indication; model both internal (Skyrizi) and external (competitor IL-23 inhibitors) cannibalization
- Skyrizi revenue ramp trajectory by indication (psoriasis, IBD, RA) and market share within IL-23 inhibitor market
- Separation timeline and completion probability; assess potential delays, tax implications, or regulatory issues that could impact valuation
- Imbruvica biosimilar penetration rates and pricing sustainability; model revenue decline rate and remaining exclusivity window
- Late-stage oncology and immunology pipeline programs with Phase III timelines; this is the key de-risking variable for post-separation growth

## Valuation Archetype: Mature Defensive Pharma (Pre-Separation) → Growth Pharma (Post-Separation)

**Primary Metrics to Track:**
- **STELARA Decline Rate** — Monitor annual decline % and attribute to internal (Skyrizi) vs. external (competitor IL-23) factors; this determines net immunology revenue trajectory
- **Skyrizi Revenue & Growth Rate** — Track absolute revenue by indication and YoY growth %; target is 20%+ growth through 2027
- **Innovative Medicine Operating Margin** — Post-separation, monitor operating margin trajectory; target is 30–35% by 2028 as portfolio matures
- **Separation Progress & Debt Allocation** — Track separation timeline, debt-to-capital ratio post-spinout, and impact on dividend capacity and capital return program
- **Pipeline Advancement** — Monitor Phase III readouts in oncology and immunology; this is the primary growth driver post-separation
