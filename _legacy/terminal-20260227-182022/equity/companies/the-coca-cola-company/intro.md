## Business Overview

Coca-Cola is a pure-play beverage company generating 61% of revenue from sparkling soft drinks, 19% from juices/plant-based, 15% from water/sports drinks, and 5% from ready-to-drink tea and coffee. The company operates a hybrid manufacture-and-franchise model where Coca-Cola produces concentrate, branded syrups, and certain finished drinks, while bottling partners (bound by exclusive agreements) handle production, distribution, and customer relationships in ~200 countries. This asset-light architecture defers capex and logistics risk to franchisees while preserving pricing power and margin control at the concentrate level.

Geographic exposure is highly diversified: North America (32% revenue) generates 40%+ operating margins on premium per-unit economics; Europe (18%) faces pricing pressure but supports margin expansion through mix shift to premium/wellness tiers; LATAM and APAC (combined 40%) exhibit emerging-market growth but lower absolute margins. The company has systematized price-in-volume trade-offs: during 2022–2024 inflation cycles, Coca-Cola achieved +8–10% organic revenue growth (80% pricing, 20% volume) while maintaining volume-decline resilience—a differentiated privilege of global iconic brands with inelastic demand curves.

Strategic direction pivots around four vectors: (1) portfolio premiumization—shifting mix from Coke Classic toward higher-margin Coke Zero, Sprite, and exotic flavors (capturing margin expansion); (2) emerging-market penetration—capex in factory automation and cold-chain logistics to drive penetration in APAC/LATAM (lower per-capita consumption = growth runway); (3) sustainability/water management—responding to regulatory and ESG pressure in water-scarce geographies; (4) margin defense via automation and procurement consolidation amid supply-chain inflation reversion.

## Revenue Architecture

Coca-Cola's waterfall reveals operational efficiency: 10% organic growth typically decomposes as +6–8% net price realization (pricing actions minus promotional cost) and -1% to +2% volume (category growth minus market-share loss/mix shift). Gross margins consistently exceed 60% given the concentrate-focused model and pricing power; operating margins run 30–35% after SG&A and logistics leverage. Bottling partner margins are ~10–15%, absorbing higher input and labor inflation—this creates pressure for franchisees to demand rebates/incentives, surfacing as "negative growth" in reported volumes.

The key distinction is *organic revenue* vs. *unit volume*: unit case volumes (the physical metric for consumption) have declined -0.5 to -2% in developed markets since 2019, but organic revenue growth remains +5–7% YoY due to pricing realization, premium mix shift, and emerging-market growth. Analysts tracking unit volume trends can detect early margin compression if pricing power weakens. LATAM and APAC growth remains 5–10% annually, with emerging-market per-capita consumption still 5–10x below US/Europe, providing long-runway growth optionality.

## Competitive Moat

Coca-Cola's moat is structural and nearly unassailable: (1) **Brand Power**—"Coca-Cola" is the world's most valuable brand (~$60B brand value), with over 90% recognition in developed markets and 60%+ in emerging markets. This permits price increases without elasticity penalties and commands premium shelf space/visibility at retail. (2) **Distribution Network**—exclusive bottler relationships and preferred placement at 25M+ retail points create switching costs for consumers and retailers. (3) **Concentrate Economics**—the franchise model transfers capex and inventory risk to bottlers while preserving 70%+ margins on concentrate sales. (4) **Scale Advantages**—procurement power (sugar, packaging, ingredients) is indexed to global scale; smaller competitors cannot achieve equivalent input-cost leverage.

Private-label carbonated soft drinks capture 25–30% of soft-drink market share in developed economies but compete only on price, not brand. Coca-Cola's pricing power implies branded drinks maintain 70% share and generate 3–4x the margin of private label. Energy/sports drinks and RTD tea/coffee are faster-growing categories, but Coca-Cola has diversified its portfolio (Monster Energy, Smartwater, Minute Maid, Honest Tea, etc.) to address category shifts, mitigating cannibalization risk.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Coca-Cola is a structural pricing-power vehicle in a post-inflation environment where input cost relief flows to the bottom line. Emerging markets (LATAM, APAC) offer 5–10 year runways of +6% volume growth as per-capita consumption normalizes toward developed-market levels. Automation capex reduces bottler cost of goods sold, enabling margin expansion and rebate moderation. The franchise model enables incremental FCF generation with minimal incremental capex; management projects $15–16B annual FCF by 2026, supporting 60%+ dividend yield with 15–20% annual buyback share reduction.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** Developed-market volume declines (-2 to -3% annually) accelerate as consumer shift toward healthier beverages (water, plant-based, functional drinks) erodes core categories. Emerging-market growth stalls if macroeconomic crises (LATAM currency devaluations, China slowdown) compress consumer spending on packaged beverages. Bottling partner consolidation and retailer power (Walmart, Carrefour, Alibaba) compress franchisee margins and demand rebates, reducing per-unit economics. Input cost inflation (sugar, PET resin, aluminum) outpaces pricing power, compressing margins. Private-label penetration expands in developed markets as consumers trade down in recessions.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Unit volume trends by geography (LATAM, APAC, North America) and category (sparkling vs. non-carbonated mix)
- Pricing realization YoY vs. inflation in COGS (sugar, PET, aluminum) and bottling labor
- Bottler margin trends and capex ratios (leading indicator of franchisee financial stress)
- Emerging-market per-capita consumption runway and macro stability (China, LATAM FX headwinds)

## Valuation Archetype: Mature Dividend Growth with Emerging-Market Exposure

**Primary Metrics to Track:**
- Organic revenue growth = volume growth + net price realization (target: +4–6% sustainable, >80% from pricing)
- Gross margin (% of revenue) YoY (rule of thumb: 60% baseline; compression <100 bps = pricing power intact)
- Developed-market unit volume churn rate (bellwether for category displacement; <-2% acceptable, >-3% signals structural headwind)
- Emerging-market organic growth rate (5–10% target for LATAM/APAC; <4% suggests macro stress)
- Free Cash Flow conversion (FCF/NOPAT; target: 90%+ reflects efficient capex and working capital)
