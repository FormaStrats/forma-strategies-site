## Business Overview

Ambev (Companhia de Bebidas das Américas) is a leading beverage company based in Brazil and operates across Latin America (Brazil, Mexico, Central America, Caribbean). The company is majority-owned by Anheuser-Busch InBev (AB InBev, Belgium-listed), which owns approximately 60% of Ambev equity. Ambev operates a diversified beverage portfolio including beer (Brahma, Skol, Antarctica), non-alcoholic beverages (Guaraná Antarctica, Sukita, water brands), and emerging categories (hard seltzers, ready-to-drink cocktails). The company operates a dominant distribution footprint across Brazil with unparalleled scale: Ambev controls approximately 60% of Brazil's beer market and maintains extensive direct-to-retail (DTR) logistics infrastructure that competitors cannot replicate.

The business is characterized by high operating leverage and stable cash generation. Beer is a mature, commodity-like category with limited product differentiation—competition centers on brand equity, distribution efficiency, and pricing power. Ambev benefits from being the market leader: the company has volume-based purchasing power with suppliers, operational scale in manufacturing and distribution, and brand portfolio diversification. However, the Brazilian market faces headwinds: economic growth is muted, consumer purchasing power is constrained (inflation, interest rates in BRL are elevated), and beer category consumption per capita is declining in developed markets as health consciousness rises. These factors create a defensive but slow-growth profile.

Ambev's strategic positioning within AB InBev creates both synergy opportunities and governance complexities. AB InBev leverages Ambev's Latin American distribution platform for emerging brand expansion (e.g., Corona, Michelob) while Ambev benefits from AB InBev's technology, procurement scale, and strategic guidance. However, minority shareholders face potential conflict of interest: AB InBev has incentives to extract cash from Ambev through dividends or transfer pricing rather than reinvest in growth. Dividend sustainability depends on maintaining beer category demand in Brazil and Ambev's competitive position within the InBev constellation.

## Revenue Architecture

Beer Revenue comprises 85-90% of total revenue and is generated from sales to distributors, wholesalers, and (in emerging channels) direct-to-consumer retailers. Ambev's pricing power is moderate: while the company leads the market, competition from subsidiaries (e.g., SABMiller brands distributed by competitors) and imports creates price discipline. Gross margins on beer are 40-50%, benefiting from scale in manufacturing and distribution. The category is mature in Brazil; volume growth is low (0-2% YoY), requiring pricing actions to drive revenue growth. Pricing has outpaced volume growth in recent years, a sign of demand elasticity and potential market share vulnerability if competitors price aggressively.

Non-Alcoholic Beverages (Guaraná, water, soft drinks) generate 10-15% of revenue and are growing faster than beer (3-5% volume growth) due to health consciousness trends. Margins on non-alcoholic beverages are lower than beer (35-45%) due to commoditized competition and lower brand equity. Emerging Categories (hard seltzers, ready-to-drink cocktails, energy drinks) are high-growth but contribute <5% of current revenue; investment in these categories is increasing but profitability is uncertain.

Net Financing Revenue comes from Ambev's captive financing operation (Ambev Finance), which provides credit to distributors and retailers. This revenue stream is stable and generates high margins but faces credit cycle risk during economic downturns.

## Competitive Moat

Ambev's moat is distribution-based and market-share driven. The company operates an unparalleled direct-to-retail (DTR) logistics network in Brazil, with direct access to ~350,000 retailers through proprietary distribution trucks and sales teams. This infrastructure creates a formidable barrier to entry: competitors cannot replicate Ambev's scale in last-mile delivery and retailer-facing services (point-of-sale systems, in-store promotions, credit offerings). The DTR network is a 15-20 year accumulated asset, and capital and time required to build a competitive network would be prohibitive for new entrants.

Brand portfolio diversification provides stability: Ambev's portfolio includes price-point products (Skol, Brahma in value segments), premium offerings (Stella Artois, Michelob), and category-adjacent products (Guaraná, water, hard seltzers). This enables the company to capture consumer spending across income levels and occasions.

However, the moat is eroding: beer consumption per capita is declining in developed markets and stalling in emerging markets; health consciousness and alternative beverages (hard seltzers, spirits, energy drinks) capture share from traditional beer. If beer category volume declines faster than Ambev can offset through pricing or category adjacencies, market share and profitability could decline. The moat is also contingent on AB InBev not abandoning the business—if AB InBev restructures or divests Ambev, the loss of scale and technology would materially weaken competitive positioning.

## Key Investment Considerations

**Bull Case:** Ambev maintains pricing power despite competitive pressures, using volume declines to justify price increases. Non-alcoholic and emerging beverage categories (hard seltzers, ready-to-drink, energy) grow faster than beer; Ambev leverages DTR network to capture incremental share. Brazilian economy improves and consumer purchasing power recovers, enabling volume growth. AB InBev maintains strategic commitment to Ambev and allocates premium brands through Ambev's distribution network, enhancing profitability. Dividend sustainability is maintained at current levels (4-5% yield) despite mature market dynamics.

**Bear Case / Primary Risks:** Beer category consumption declines faster than consensus expects due to health consciousness and category fragmentation. Ambev loses market share to lower-cost competitors or imported brands, compressing margins. Pricing actions fail to offset volume declines, forcing margin compression or cash flow deterioration. Brazilian economic growth remains weak, pressuring consumer demand and pricing power. AB InBev restructures operations or divests Ambev, triggering shareholder losses. Dividend is cut, repricing the equity as a lower-yielding equity. Currency headwinds (BRL depreciation) compress U.S. dollar valuations.

**Due Diligence Focus Areas:**
- Beer category volume trends in Brazil and Latin America; assess structural decline vs. cyclical pause
- Ambev market share in beer by geography and price segment; benchmark vs. competitors (Heineken, SABMiller subsidiaries)
- Pricing realization and volume elasticity; model scenario analysis for pricing vs. volume trade-offs
- Non-alcoholic and emerging category growth rates and margin profiles; assess whether cannibalizes beer or incremental
- AB InBev strategic intentions and integration plans; assess likelihood of divestment or restructuring
- Dividend coverage ratio and free cash flow stability; project sustainability at current yield levels
- Currency exposure (BRL/USD) and impact of depreciation on consolidated results
